Outsource social media or hire in-house?

Most owners reach this point the same way. Posting "when there's a free minute" stopped working months ago, the account is half-dead, and you are tired of feeling guilty about it. So you either hire someone or hand it to an outside team. Both can work fine. Picking the wrong one usually costs a few months and a fair bit of money.

First, list the actual jobs

"Doing social media" sounds like one task. In practice, for a business that wants customers out of it, it is closer to eight:

  • a strategy and a content plan for the month;
  • photos and short videos, both filming and editing;
  • graphics for posts, stories and covers;
  • captions, hooks and video scripts;
  • publishing on schedule across platforms;
  • answering comments and DMs;
  • setting up and tuning paid ads;
  • a report that says what brought leads and sales.

That is five or six different skills. So the real question is less "agency or employee" and more "who can cover all of this for money I can actually spend".

The case for hiring

Someone in-house knows your product inside out and is around all day. They can react to a trend the same afternoon and film in your shop every morning without booking anything.

The downsides are real, though. One person who is equally good at video, design, copy and ads is rare, so you often end up paying freelancers on top. Salary plus taxes, equipment and apps adds up to a lot more than the number in the job ad. Hiring and training take one to three months, and if that person leaves, you are back at zero. You also have nothing to compare their results against.

The case for outsourcing

With an agency you get several specialists for roughly the cost of one employee. Work starts in days. The team has already tested ideas on other clients, so you are not paying for their learning curve. And it is easy to do more before a launch and less in a slow season.

What you give up: the team is not standing in your store, so somebody on your side will need to send photos and clips, or you book shooting days. Reacting to something that blew up an hour ago can be slower. And agencies vary wildly in quality, so you have to choose carefully.

Quick checklist

Outsourcing probably fits if most of these sound like you:

  • your budget is below what a full-time hire really costs;
  • you need video, design and ads, not just someone pressing "post";
  • you want to see results in the next month or two;
  • social media is one sales channel among several.

Hiring probably fits if:

  • social media is your main source of sales and runs every single day;
  • you make a lot of content on site, like a restaurant, salon or shop;
  • you can pay for a small team, or one person plus outside help;
  • you have time to manage and train them.

The mix a lot of our clients end up with

What often works best is a split. The owner or a staff member films short raw clips on a phone during the day. The outside team takes it from there: plan, edit, design, captions, publishing and ads. You keep real footage from your business and still get a professional result, without four new salaries.

Trying an agency without much risk

  • Start with a one-month pilot instead of a yearly contract.
  • Agree on two or three numbers that matter: leads, booked calls, sales.
  • Ask to see work for businesses like yours.
  • Find out who will actually be working on your account.
  • Ask if they would take part of their fee from the sales they bring. A team that trusts its own work often says yes. More on that in how pay-for-performance works.

Our take

If you need several skills, quickly, on a small-business budget, outsourcing usually comes out ahead. Once social media becomes a daily sales engine and the budget grows, move part of it in-house and keep outside help for the rest.
