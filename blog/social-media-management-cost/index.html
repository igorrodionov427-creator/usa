How much does social media management cost in 2026?

Ask five agencies what they charge to run your Instagram and you will get five different answers, most of them after a "quick discovery call". We get why owners find this frustrating. So here is the honest version: what people actually pay in 2026, why the quotes swing so much, and where the money tends to leak.

Rough price ranges

These are ballpark numbers for the US and Western Europe, pulled from public price lists of freelancers and agencies. Your city, niche and content volume will move them up or down.

OptionTypical monthly costWhat you usually get
Freelancer, 1–2 platforms$400–$1,5008–15 posts a month, basic captions, scheduling
Small agency, 2–3 platforms$1,200–$4,000Content plan, design, short videos, comment replies, monthly report
Full-service agency$3,000–$10,000+Strategy, video production, paid ads, influencer work
In-house social media manager$3,500–$6,500+ salary, plus taxes and toolsOne person's time, usually no designer or editor

One thing people often miss: the ad budget you pay to Meta, TikTok or Google almost always comes on top of the management fee.

Why two quotes can differ five times

How many platforms. Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok each want their own format. We would rather do two platforms properly than spread a small budget across five. Two good ones usually bring more customers anyway.

Video. Reels, TikTok and Shorts are where most free reach lives right now. They are also the slowest thing to make. Filming, cutting, captions, maybe a voiceover. Video alone can double a package price, so ask how many clips per month are included and who is holding the camera.

Who makes the content. "We post the photos you send" and "we plan, shoot, design and write everything" are two very different jobs. The second costs more. It also gives you back the hours you wanted back in the first place.

Replies to comments and DMs. This is the bit where followers turn into buyers, and plenty of cheap packages leave it out. If people message you on Instagram, somebody should answer within hours.

Reporting. A monthly report should show leads and sales, not just reach. If nobody is counting customers, you cannot tell whether you are paying for results or for noise.

Costs nobody mentions upfront

  • Tools: scheduling, design and analytics apps can add $50–$300 a month if they are on your card.
  • Stock footage and music licensed for commercial use.
  • Setup fees for audits, brand guides and cleaning up old accounts.
  • Long contracts. Six or twelve months locked in before you know if it works is a risk you take, not them.
  • Your own time. Approving posts, sending photos, answering questions. A decent partner keeps this to a few minutes a week.

Freelancer, agency or a hire?

A freelancer is the cheapest way in. The catch is that one person rarely does strategy, design, video and ads equally well, and if they get sick, your page goes quiet.

An agency costs more but gives you a team that does not disappear. For small businesses, small agencies are often the sweet spot because the senior people actually touch your account.

Hiring in-house makes sense once social media is a main sales channel and you can pay for more than one person. We go through this choice in outsourcing vs. hiring in-house.

Paying for customers instead of posts

The usual deal is a fixed fee for a fixed number of posts. But posts were never the point. Customers were.

That is why more agencies now offer performance-based pricing, where they earn a cut of the sales they bring. We do this at Apolline for projects where it fits: we find the customers, you get the order, and only then you pay us a percentage. No orders, no fee. The details are in how pay-for-performance marketing works.

Ask these before you sign anything

  1. How many posts and videos per month, and on which platforms?
  2. Who creates the content, and how much of my time does it need?
  3. Are comments and direct messages part of the deal?
  4. What will you report on? Does that include leads and sales?
  5. What is the minimum term, and how do I leave?
  6. Is ad spend included or separate?

So what should you budget?

For most small businesses, professional social media management in 2026 lands somewhere around $1,000 to $4,000 a month, plus ads. Our advice: fewer platforms, done well, with video, and judged by leads rather than likes.
