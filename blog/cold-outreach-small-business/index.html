Cold outreach that gets replies, not spam flags

For a lot of B2B businesses, the quickest route to new clients is not waiting to be found. It's reaching out to the right people yourself. Do it badly and you burn your domain and your name. Do it well and your calendar fills with calls that are actually relevant.

This is how we approach it.

Start with a short, sharp list

The classic mistake is writing to everyone. Narrow it down: industry, company size, location, the role of whoever decides, and ideally a trigger that suggests they need you right now. A new location, a hiring spree, a product launch, a run of bad reviews.

Two hundred well-picked contacts will beat five thousand random ones every time.

Make sure the emails arrive

If you land in spam, nothing else on this page matters.

  • Send from a separate domain that looks like your main one, so your main domain stays clean.
  • Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on it.
  • Warm new mailboxes up slowly before any campaign.
  • Keep volume low: a few dozen emails per mailbox per day.
  • Check addresses before sending so you don't rack up bounces.
  • Keep the first email plain text, without a pile of links or images.

Write like a person, and keep it short

A good first email fits on a phone screen. Roughly:

  • one line that shows you looked at them specifically;
  • one line on the problem you solve, in their words;
  • one line of proof, ideally a result for a similar company;
  • one easy question, like "Worth a 15-minute call next week?"

A template we like:

Hi [Name] — saw you opened a second studio in [city] last month, congrats. That's usually when Instagram messages and bookings start piling up. We set up short videos and an auto-reply bot for [similar client] and [their result]. Up for a quick call next week to see if something like that fits?

Fill the brackets with real details and a real result. No attachments, no company history, and please no "I hope this email finds you well".

Follow up, politely

Most replies come to a follow-up, not to the first email. Plan two or three, a few days apart, and make each one add something: a short case, a useful idea, a simple yes-or-no question. Then stop.

Bring in LinkedIn

For B2B, pairing email with LinkedIn tends to work better than email alone. View their profile, connect with a short note (no pitch), react to something they posted, then send a message.

Know the rules where you send

They differ by country, so check what applies to you.

  • In the US, CAN-SPAM wants honest headers and subject lines, your physical address and a working way to opt out.
  • In the EU and UK, GDPR and e-privacy rules apply. B2B outreach usually leans on legitimate interest, has to be relevant to the person's job, and needs an easy opt-out. Some countries are stricter than others.

Unsubscribe requests get honored immediately, no exceptions. And this isn't legal advice; if you're unsure, ask a lawyer in your market.

Watch the numbers that mean something

  • Open rates are unreliable these days because of privacy features. Don't obsess over them.
  • Reply rate, and especially positive reply rate, tells you if the message works.
  • Booked calls and closed deals are the real score.

Change one thing at a time: the subject, the first line, the offer.
