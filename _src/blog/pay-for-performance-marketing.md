Most marketing is sold by the hour or by the month. You pay for the posts, the ads and the report whether a single customer shows up or not. Pay-for-performance flips that. The agency gets paid when you get paid, usually as a percentage of the sales it brings in.

It sounds too good to be true, and for some businesses it is not a fit. For others it is the most sensible way to work with an agency. Here is how to tell which one you are.

## How it works

1. The agency brings in potential customers, through social media, ads, outreach or marketplaces.
2. One of them places an order or pays for a booking.
3. You pay the agency the agreed percentage of that order, or a fixed fee per qualified lead.

No orders, no fee for results. Some agencies pair a small monthly base with a commission; others, for the right projects, work on commission alone.

## Ways to structure the payment

- **A percentage of revenue** from sales you can attribute to the agency. Common in e-commerce and in services with clear prices.
- **A fixed fee per lead**: a booked call, a filled form, a qualified message.
- **A fixed fee per sale**, which works when your average order is fairly stable.
- **A hybrid**: a low base that covers production, plus commission.

Which one makes sense mostly depends on how easily you can see where each customer came from.

## When it's a good fit

It tends to work when:

- you can trace every customer to a source, whether a promo code, a dedicated link, a bot or a separate phone number;
- your margin still leaves you profit after the commission;
- you can actually handle more orders without drowning;
- people decide to buy within weeks, not over a year.

It works less well for brand-new products nobody has bought yet, very long B2B sales cycles, or businesses with no way to track where customers come from.

## Put these in writing first

- **Attribution.** What counts as a sale "from the agency" (links, codes, the bot, a CRM field), and for how long after first contact.
- **The rate.** The percentage, whether it's taken from revenue or profit, and whether repeat orders count.
- **Refunds.** Usually, if the order is returned, the commission is reversed.
- **Reporting.** A shared sheet or dashboard you can both see.
- **When you pay.** Monthly, for example, after the customer has paid you.
- **Ad spend.** If ads are involved, who covers them.

A one-page agreement is plenty. Clear rules keep everyone friendly.

## Why would an agency agree to this?

Because it keeps them honest about where they spend effort. An agency paid from your sales cares about leads and orders, not about how many posts went out this week. It also means they only take on projects they believe they can deliver. Which is a handy filter for you too.
