Boosting a post for $20 and hoping for the best is how most small businesses meet Meta ads. Usually nothing much happens, and the conclusion is "ads don't work for us". They often do. They just need a clear goal, a sensible budget and creatives made for the feed.

## How much should you spend?

There is no magic number, but here's how we think about it:

- **Testing:** around $10–$30 a day for two to four weeks gives Meta enough data to learn and gives you enough results to judge. Less than that and you're mostly guessing.
- **Working budget:** once you know your cost per lead or per sale, scale what works gradually, about 20% at a time.
- **Rule of thumb:** work backwards from what a customer is worth. If a new client brings you $300 in profit, paying $40 to get one is a good deal; paying $400 is not.

Remember the ad budget goes to Meta. Creating the ads and managing them is a separate cost, whether it's your time or an agency's fee.

## Pick one goal per campaign

Meta optimises for whatever you tell it to. Choose the objective that matches what you actually want:

- **Leads** if you want inquiries: a form inside Facebook/Instagram or messages to your inbox.
- **Sales** if you have an online shop with the Meta pixel or Conversions API set up.
- **Messages** if customers usually buy after a chat on WhatsApp, Messenger or Instagram DMs.
- **Traffic** only if the landing page itself does the selling.

"Boost post" buttons are fine for reach, but a proper campaign set up in Ads Manager gives you much more control.

## Audience: simpler than it used to be

Meta's automated targeting (Advantage+ audiences) has become good at finding buyers when the creative and the goal are clear. For a local business:

- set the location tightly: your city or a radius around you;
- use age or language limits only when they truly apply;
- add interests as suggestions, not hard walls;
- retarget people who visited your site or engaged with your profile; they're the cheapest customers you'll get.

## Creatives matter more than settings

In 2026, the ad itself does most of the targeting. What works for small businesses:

- **Short vertical video** (9:16): a real person, the product or the result in the first two seconds, captions on.
- **Before and after**, real customer results, real prices.
- **A clear offer:** "Free consultation", "Book this week", "Delivery tomorrow".
- **Several versions.** Run three to five creatives at once and let the results show which one wins.

Stock photos and corporate slogans rarely survive in a feed full of real people.

## Get the basics right before you spend

- Install the Meta pixel (and Conversions API if possible) on your site so Meta can see leads and sales.
- Make sure the landing page or form loads fast on a phone and asks for as little as possible.
- Have someone ready to reply to leads within minutes. A lead called back the next day is half a lead.

## Reading the results

Ignore vanity numbers. Watch:

- **cost per lead or cost per purchase;**
- **lead quality:** how many leads turn into actual customers;
- **click-through rate** on each creative, to spot which ones people care about;
- **frequency:** if the same people see the ad too often, refresh the creative.

Give each test at least a week before changing things. Tweaking every day resets Meta's learning and wastes money.
