"How much is a website?" is a bit like "how much is a car?". It depends on what you need it to do. But you can still get a realistic range before talking to anyone, and know which questions to ask so the final bill doesn't surprise you.

The ranges below are ballpark figures for the US and Western Europe in 2026, based on public price lists. Your quotes will vary by region and complexity.

## Three ways to get a website

| Option | Typical upfront cost | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| DIY website builder | $0–$500, plus $15–$50 a month | A simple site you're happy to build and update yourself |
| Freelancer | $1,000–$5,000 | A small business site with a few pages and a contact form |
| Agency or small studio | $3,000–$15,000+ | Custom design, more pages, booking, shop, copy and SEO done for you |

An online shop or a site with bookings, payments or integrations usually sits at the higher end of each range.

## What actually drives the price

- **Number of pages.** A one-page site and a 20-page site with a page per service are very different jobs.
- **Design.** A tweaked template is cheaper than a custom design made for your brand.
- **Copywriting and photos.** Many quotes assume you supply all the text and images. Writing them is real work, and it's often what makes a site sell.
- **Features.** Online booking, payments, a shop, multiple languages, a customer area or CRM connections all add time.
- **SEO groundwork.** Page structure, titles, speed, schema and a Google Business Profile link-up cost a little more now and save a lot later.
- **Who maintains it.** Some prices include updates and fixes; others charge for every change.

## Yearly running costs

Budget for these whether you build it yourself or not:

- **Domain:** roughly $10–$30 a year.
- **Hosting or builder subscription:** from $0 for simple static sites to $20–$50 a month for builders and managed hosting.
- **Email on your domain:** a few dollars per user per month.
- **Maintenance and updates:** from nothing (a simple static site) to a monthly retainer for bigger sites.
- **Paid plugins, themes or apps,** if your platform uses them.

## What a small business site should include

Whatever you pay, make sure you get:

1. A clear first screen: what you do, for whom, and one obvious button (call, book, message).
2. A page or section for each main service, with prices or "from" prices if you can.
3. Real photos, reviews and examples of work.
4. Contact details everywhere, plus a quick way to reach you: WhatsApp, a form, booking.
5. Fast loading and a layout that works perfectly on phones, where most visitors are.
6. The basics of SEO and a link to your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization/).
7. A privacy policy and the legal pages your country requires.

## Questions to ask before you pay

- Is the price fixed, and exactly what does it include?
- Who writes the text and provides the photos?
- How many rounds of changes are included?
- Will I own the site and the domain?
- What does it cost per year to keep it running?
- How fast will it load on a phone, and will it be ready for Google?
- What happens if I need a change in six months?

## Hourly billing vs. a fixed price

Hourly billing sounds flexible but makes the final number impossible to predict. We prefer a fixed price: we agree the pages, features and content up front, and the price goes into the agreement. If you want something extra later, you get a separate quote before anything changes.

## Bottom line

For most small businesses in 2026, a professional website that actually brings inquiries costs somewhere around **$1,500 to $6,000** upfront, plus a modest yearly amount to keep it running. Spend on clear copy, real photos and a fast mobile experience before fancy animations.
