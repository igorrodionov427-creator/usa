Most small-business Instagram accounts look busy and sell almost nothing. Posts go out, a few friends like them, and the owner quietly wonders whether any of it is worth the time. Usually it is, but only once the account is set up to do one job: get the right people to message you.

Here is how we approach Instagram for small businesses, step by step.

## Start with the profile, not the posts

People decide in a few seconds whether to follow or leave, and they decide on your profile page. Before you post anything else, check these:

- **Name field.** It is searchable, so add what you do: "Nora's Bakery | Custom Cakes Austin" beats "Nora's".
- **Bio.** One line on who you help, one line on what makes you different, one clear next step ("DM 'CAKE' for a quote").
- **Link.** Your website, a booking page or a simple link page. Make sure it works on a phone.
- **Contact buttons.** Switch to a professional account and add email, phone or a booking button.
- **Highlights.** Four or five covers people actually need: prices, work examples, reviews, how to order, location.

If a stranger can't tell what you sell and how to buy within five seconds, fix that first.

## Decide what the account is for

"Grow followers" is not a goal. Pick one of these and let it shape everything:

- **Bookings** for a salon, clinic or studio.
- **Orders** for a shop or bakery.
- **Inquiries** for a service business: builders, agencies, consultants.
- **Foot traffic** for a café or a local store.

A salon posts before-and-afters and a "book now" link. A B2B service posts short explanations and case stories. Same platform, very different content.

## What to post

A simple rotation that works for most local and small businesses:

1. **Reels** two or three times a week. They are still the main way Instagram shows you to people who don't follow you yet. Our [short-form video playbook](/blog/short-form-video-for-small-business/) covers what to film.
2. **Carousels** once a week: a tip, a price breakdown, "5 things to know before…". People save these, and saves tell Instagram the post was useful.
3. **Stories** most days: behind the scenes, today's slots, a poll, a quick question. Stories keep you in front of the people who already follow you.

Plan it a month ahead so you are not inventing posts at 11 p.m. Our [30-day content calendar guide](/blog/social-media-content-calendar/) shows how.

## Direct messages are where the money is

On Instagram, sales almost always happen in DMs. A few rules:

- Reply within an hour during working hours if you possibly can. A lead that waits half a day often books somewhere else.
- Use saved replies for prices, hours and location, then add one personal line.
- Ask one simple question that moves things forward: "Which date works for you?"
- Use keyword prompts in captions ("comment PRICE") and answer each one in DMs. If you get many, an automated reply or a [chatbot](/blog/chatbot-for-small-business/) helps.

## Hashtags, captions and timing

- Hashtags matter less than they used to. A handful of specific ones (your niche plus your city) is enough.
- Write captions for people, not the algorithm. A strong first line, a bit of context, one call to action.
- Post when your audience is online. After a few weeks, check the times in Insights and adjust.

## Collaborations and local reach

Two quick wins small businesses skip:

- **Collab posts** with a neighbour business or a local creator. The post shows on both accounts.
- **Location tags and local hashtags** on every post. People search "brunch Lisbon" and "nails Manchester" more than you'd think.

## The numbers worth watching

Followers and likes are nice. These tell you whether Instagram is paying off:

- reach from non-followers (are new people seeing you?);
- profile visits and link taps;
- DMs and replies to Stories;
- bookings, orders or inquiries you can trace back to Instagram (ask new customers where they found you).

Check them once a month, keep what brings messages, drop what doesn't.
