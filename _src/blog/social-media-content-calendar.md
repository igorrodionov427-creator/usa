"What do I post today?" That one question quietly kills most small-business accounts. Without a plan, posting depends on mood and spare time, and you run out of both by Wednesday. A content calendar means you make the decisions once and then just follow them.

This is the method we use with clients. It takes one afternoon and covers a full month.

## 1. Pick one goal for the month

More inquiries. Bookings for a new service. Selling a particular product. Building an audience before a launch. Choose one. Every post should push towards it, directly or not.

## 2. Settle on three or four themes

We call them pillars, but they are really just themes you keep coming back to. For most small businesses these four cover it:

1. **Know-how**: tips, how-tos, answers to customer questions.
2. **Proof**: results, before and after, reviews, short case stories.
3. **Behind the scenes**: the team, the process, the workshop or office.
4. **The offer**: products, prices, deals, and a clear "here's how to buy".

A rough mix that works: about 40% know-how, 25% proof, 20% behind the scenes, 15% offer. Sell in every post and people stop listening. Never sell and nobody buys.

## 3. Choose a pace you can keep

Pick something you can sustain for months, not a heroic first week:

- Instagram: 3–4 posts or Reels a week, plus stories.
- TikTok and YouTube Shorts: 3–5 short videos a week (the same clips work for both).
- A Facebook page: 2–3 posts a week.
- LinkedIn for B2B: 2–3 posts a week.
- A Telegram or WhatsApp channel: 2–4 updates a week.

Two platforms you show up on reliably beat five you visit now and then.

## 4. Use one weekly template, four times

| Day | Format | Theme | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Short video | Know-how | "3 mistakes people make when choosing…" |
| Wednesday | Carousel or photo | Proof | Before and after, with a short story |
| Friday | Short video | Behind the scenes | An order being made, sped up |
| Sunday | Post + story | Offer | This week's deal and how to get it |

Four weeks of that is sixteen pieces of content. For most small businesses, that's plenty.

## 5. Brainstorm the topics in one sitting

Give yourself half an hour and write 20 topics:

- the 10 questions customers ask you most;
- 5 recent jobs or results you can show;
- 3 things about how you work that people find surprising;
- 2 offers or promotions for the month.

Drop each one into a slot in the calendar.

## 6. Make things in batches

Making content day by day is exhausting. Instead:

- film two weeks' worth of video in one session;
- design all the graphics at once from templates;
- write every caption in a single document.

That turns a daily chore into two or three focused sessions a month.

## 7. Schedule the whole month

Load everything into a scheduler so it goes out on its own at the right times. Keep a couple of slots open for news, trends and whatever comes up.

## 8. Look back at the end of the month

Check what actually brought messages, bookings and sales, not just likes. Keep the formats that worked, drop the rest, and plan next month with that in mind.
