If you only do one thing on social media this year, make it short vertical video. Reels, TikTok and Shorts all show videos to people who have never heard of you, which ordinary posts mostly don't. For a small business that is about the cheapest way to get in front of new customers.

The obvious problem is time. You run a business, not a film studio. Below is the way we set this up for clients so it fits into a normal week.

## Make one video, post it three times

All three platforms take the same thing: vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, ideally 15 to 60 seconds. So shoot once and publish everywhere, changing only the caption. Strip other apps' watermarks before you re-post; the platforms are widely reported to push watermarked clips less.

One solid video on three platforms will do more for you than three rushed ones.

## Eight ideas that work for nearly any business

1. Before and after: a repair, a haircut, a cleaned car, a finished sign.
2. The process, sped up. How the thing gets made or the job gets done.
3. A customer question, answered. "How long does it take?" "Why is it so expensive?" "Which one should I pick?"
4. A common myth in your field, and what is actually true.
5. A mistake customers make and how to avoid it.
6. Behind the scenes: packing orders, the team, the workshop.
7. A price breakdown that shows what people are really paying for.
8. A real result or customer story, with their permission.

If you don't know where to start, start with number 3. Write down the ten questions you hear most often. That's ten videos.

## The first two seconds

People decide almost immediately whether to keep watching. So skip the "hi guys" and the logo animation and open with the best moment:

- show the finished result first, then rewind to how you got there;
- or open with a pointed question, like "Why do most small shops lose money on Instagram?";
- put a short line of text on screen, because lots of people scroll with the sound off.

For the same reason, always burn captions into the video.

## A week that takes about two hours

- **Monday, 20 minutes.** Pick three topics from your list.
- **Tuesday, 40 minutes.** Film all three in one go on your phone, by a window or with a cheap light. Several short takes are easier than one long one.
- **Wednesday.** Edit, add captions and music. Do it yourself in a simple app or hand the clips off.
- **The rest of the week.** Post one video every couple of days when your audience is online, and answer every comment in the first hour.

Keeping it going matters far more than making it perfect. Three ordinary videos a week for three months will beat one beautiful video a month.

## Length, captions, posting

- Aim for 15–35 seconds unless the story genuinely holds up longer.
- The caption needs one line of context and one clear ask, e.g. "DM us PRICE for a quote".
- Use a handful of relevant hashtags, not thirty.
- After a few weeks, check the analytics to see when your people are actually online.

## What to watch in the numbers

Views are nice. They don't pay rent. Keep an eye on:

- watch time and how many people finish the video;
- shares and saves, which tell you it was useful;
- profile visits, link clicks and DMs, which tell you it brings customers;
- leads and sales you can trace back to a specific video.
