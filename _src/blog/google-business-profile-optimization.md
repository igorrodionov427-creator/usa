When someone searches "dentist near me" or "pizza in Valencia", the first thing they usually see is not a website. It's the map with three businesses under it. For a local business, getting into those three spots can matter more than anything else you do online, and the tool for it is free: your Google Business Profile.

This is the checklist we run through for clients.

## 1. Claim and verify the profile

Search for your business on Google Maps. If it's there, claim it; if not, add it. Verification can be by phone, email, video or a postcard, depending on your business and country. Nothing else on this list works until the profile is verified.

## 2. Get the basics exactly right

- **Name**: your real business name, as it appears on your sign. Adding keywords to the name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
- **Address or service area**: a shop with customers coming in shows its address; a plumber who drives to clients sets a service area instead.
- **Phone and website**: the same details everywhere online, on your site, social profiles and directories. Inconsistent details confuse both Google and customers.
- **Hours**, including holiday hours. Few things annoy people more than arriving at a closed door that Google said was open.

## 3. Choose categories carefully

The primary category is one of the strongest signals Google uses for local ranking. Pick the most specific one that fits ("Cosmetic dentist" rather than "Dentist" if that is what you mainly do), then add a few secondary categories for the other things you offer. Look at which categories the top-ranking competitors use.

## 4. Fill in services and products

Add every service you offer, with a short description and, where it makes sense, a price or "from" price. For shops, add your main products with photos. This gives Google more text to match against searches and answers questions before people even call.

## 5. Photos that make people choose you

Profiles with good photos get more clicks and more direction requests. Upload:

- the outside of the building, so people recognise it when they arrive;
- the inside and the team;
- your work or products, close up and in good light;
- a clean logo and a cover photo.

Add new photos regularly. A profile whose newest photo is from 2021 looks abandoned.

## 6. Write a description that sells

You get 750 characters. Say what you do, who it's for, where you work and what makes you different. Write it for people, mention your main service and area naturally, and leave out links and promotional fluff.

## 7. Post updates

Google lets you publish posts on the profile: offers, news, events, a new service. They won't transform your ranking on their own, but they show that the business is alive and give people one more reason to call. Once a week or every two weeks is plenty.

## 8. Reviews: the factor you can influence most

Number of reviews, how recent they are and your average rating all affect both ranking and whether people pick you. Ask every happy customer, reply to every review, and handle bad ones calmly. We wrote a separate guide on [how to get more Google reviews the right way](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## 9. Turn on messaging and bookings, if you can answer fast

Where available, enabling messages or a booking link removes a step for the customer. Only switch it on if someone will reply quickly; unanswered messages hurt more than no button at all.

## 10. Make your website back it up

Google cross-checks your profile with your site. Your website should show the same name, address and phone, list your services, and ideally have a page for each main service and area you cover. If you don't have a site yet, see [what a small business website costs](/blog/small-business-website-cost/).

## How long until it works?

Fixing categories, photos and details can show results within weeks. Reviews and local authority build over months. The businesses that win in Maps are rarely the biggest. They're the ones whose profile is complete, active and full of recent reviews.
